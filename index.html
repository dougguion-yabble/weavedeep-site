Every company is full of knowledge that never reaches the person who needs it.

The answer almost always exists somewhere: three teams over, in a file with a name you would never think to search, in a conversation that happened before you arrived. It simply never reaches the person who needs it while it still matters.

The same question, asked again.

None of it is anyone's failing. There has simply never been a way for an organization to hold everything it knows in one place and reason about it the way a single well-informed person would, if only one person could ever know that much. A wiki goes stale the week it is written. Search finds the document but not the answer buried inside it. And the person who understood how the pieces fit has, more often than not, already moved on and taken the map with them.

It learns your organization from what is already there, and then it answers.

It reads the systems you already use and builds its own understanding from what exists, so there is no second system to curate, no wiki to keep writing and no discipline to keep up. That matters, because every knowledge system that depends on people remembering to update it eventually dies of neglect.

Ask it a question and it answers the way the most knowledgeable person in the building would, in plain language and to the point, except that it has read everything and forgets nothing. It is not searching for documents and handing them back for you to sift; it is drawing on an understanding it has already built of how your organization actually works. And because it knows where every part of that understanding came from, it can show you the reasoning and the exact record behind any claim the moment you want to see it.

Memory, Mind, and Soul.

Most software gives you somewhere to put information and stops there, as if what a company knows could be solved with a bigger filing cabinet. Weave is built from three parts that feed one another, and the one everyone pictures first, the place things are kept, turns out to be the least interesting of the three.

Memory

Everything your organization knows, read from the tools you already use so you never load it by hand. When your records change, Weave re-reads them, and there is no second copy for anyone to keep current.

Mind

The understanding Weave builds on top of that memory and rebuilds as the record changes, so it reasons over what is current rather than a stale snapshot. This is the part that reasons, connects distant things, and answers in plain language.

Soul

The character the Mind reasons inside of: who each team is, what it owns, how it actually works, and what it holds to be good. A mind without it is a brilliant stranger. The Soul is what makes the answers sound like your organization and respect its judgment, rather than a capable machine's. It is the part that makes this yours, and not merely clever.

What Weave is, and what it is not.

Most people land here curious and with nothing to compare Weave to, which usually means comparing it to the wrong thing. These are the questions that tend to come next, answered plainly.

So what is this, really?

Picture a brilliant new colleague who has somehow read every document, message, and decision your company has ever produced, remembers all of it, and understands how it all fits together, and who you can simply ask. That is the nearest thing to Weave. Underneath, though, it is not a model that was trained and asked to do everything, and it is not search dressed up as conversation. It is a system, an architecture of parts each engineered for one job: one reads and holds your organization's record, one builds a living understanding of it and rebuilds it as the record changes, one keeps that understanding grounded in who your teams actually are, and others trace every answer to its source and flag sensitive things for a person to clear. It can do these things because it was deliberately built to, not because a general model was coaxed into approximating them, which is why Weave is a real, inspectable thing you own rather than a clever trick you rent.

Is this just a chatbot?

No, and the difference is the whole point. A chatbot is a clever talker with no particular stake in the truth of what it says. Weave speaks only from your real records, shows the evidence behind every answer, and tells you when it does not know rather than filling the silence. And it does more than answer the literal question: when you bring it something, it also surfaces the related thing across the organization that you didn’t think to ask about.

How is it different from ChatGPT or Copilot?

Those are brilliant generalists. They know an astonishing amount about the world and almost nothing about your organization, and when they do not know something about your business they will often produce a confident answer anyway. Weave is the opposite kind of thing. It knows your organization specifically and deeply, it speaks only from what your records support, and it hands you the source for every claim, so you are never guessing whether to believe it.

Does it make things up?

This is the right thing to worry about, and it is the problem Weave was built around. It answers only from your records, and every claim it makes is checked against those records before you see it, and whatever the record will not support is revised or cut rather than shown. When the answer genuinely is not there, it says so plainly instead of inventing something to be helpful.

Do we have to put everything into it, or change how we work?

No. That is usually the moment these systems fail, when they ask everyone to do a little extra forever and the extra quietly stops happening. Weave reads what your organization already creates in the course of normal work. Nobody has to tag, file, summarize or maintain anything, because there is no second library to keep: Weave reads your real work and re-reads it as it changes.

Will it touch or change our files?

Never. Weave only reads. It connects in a way that makes writing to your systems impossible rather than merely against the rules, so it cannot alter, move or delete a single thing you own. Whatever it learns, your original files are exactly as they were.

Who can see what? Is our sensitive material safe?

Sensitive material is treated as sensitive by default. Weave flags anything that looks personal, confidential or restricted and puts it in front of a person to rule on before it can go out, so nothing delicate reaches the wrong audience on its own say-so. Your records and the memory Weave builds live in stores you own, and what Weave learns for one organization is never mixed with another.

Isn't this just search, or a better wiki?

Search and wikis both stop at storage. Search can find a document but cannot read it for you or connect it to the three other things that make it matter, and a wiki only knows what someone found time to write down, which is why it is out of date almost immediately. Weave understands rather than stores. It reads across everything, builds an understanding instead of a pile of files and re-reads your records rather than asking you to keep a wiki alive by hand, and answers the actual question instead of pointing at where the answer might be.

What happens when the AI models keep changing?

Weave is built so it is never welded to one model or provider. No model is named in its code; it is designed to run each task on whichever model has passed its own evaluation, and to move to a better one once that one proves out, so your organization's understanding is not tied to one company's roadmap. Today one provider carries the load; the machinery to switch between proven models is built and waiting on the first pilot. As the field improves, Weave is built to improve with it, without a rebuild.

Can I actually see it work?

Yes. The best way to understand Weave is to sit with a real one and watch it think through a question, evidence and all. That is what a first conversation is for, and it tends to do more in ten minutes than any amount of description on a page like this one.

How do we get it, and what does it cost?

Getting one is a process Weave runs, not something a backroom team hand-assembles. It reads your organization's own history, the documents, decisions, messages and systems you already have, and forms its understanding from them. The whole thing is engineered, automated, and properly tested in the real world, so there is no one behind a curtain piecing your Weave together. The decisions along the way are yours; the work itself is done by a system you can inspect. And because it is a system rather than a person, anywhere it falls short can be found, corrected, and then relied on, which is more than can be said for work that depends on someone remembering to get it right. For a company of fifty to two hundred-plus people it starts at $50,000, paid once, and then it is yours to keep, with an optional monthly Sentinel subscription and, as the network grows, a connection to other Weaves that you can take or leave. We take on only a few organizations at a time while the process proves itself in the open, so the next step is a conversation rather than a signup.

What happens if we stop, or you walk away?

You keep everything. Your records and the memory Weave builds sit in stores you own and control, and there is nothing to migrate back because it was always yours. The aim is to leave your organization more capable than it was, not more dependent.
